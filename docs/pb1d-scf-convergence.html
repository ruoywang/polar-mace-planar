Convergence of the lagged solvent field

Model rho1d-w10 · 80 charged frames (40 val + 40 test) · one process, one model — only the solvent-field state differs between modes

1 · What is being compared

Each forward pass solves the 1-D PB problem fresh on the final charge density; the lagged part is only the solvent field that polarises the MACE charges (stage 1). Three field states are compared: the first-encounter path (empty cache: stage-1 field from a prior-only solve on the unpolarised density), one repeat (second forward, field = own previous solution), and the converged training field (the structure's end-of-training cache row, read-only).

3 · Per-structure deviation from the converged field

Electrode-potential deviation per structure (hover for values). First encounter (orange): rms 8.1 meV, max 25.6 meV. After one repeat (blue): rms 0.5 meV, max 2.2 meV — the lagged-field fixed point converges in a single iteration. Fermi-level deviations are numerically identical to the potential deviations (the field enters the Fermi level only through the potential).

4 · Practical consequence

A brand-new structure can be predicted in one forward pass at a 2–5 meV penalty, or in two passes at full training-time fidelity. Validation and test structures during training are handled by the same mechanism and need no special treatment: their cached fields contain only the model's own outputs, so no label information leaks.

5 · Update: training without the lagged field at all (fresh stage 1)

2026-08-15 · 34-epoch A/B gate + 500-epoch production (6-no_old) against the lagged reference (4-TTF), same data and seed. The production run also carries the occupancy-head auxiliary supervision.

The follow-up question to this page: if the first-encounter penalty is this small, can training drop the cross-epoch cache entirely? A switch (solvent_pb1d_fresh_stage1) now recomputes the stage-1 field from the pre-SCF density on every forward — training steps, evaluation, and MD run the identical path, and the cache directory stays empty (verified: 0 files over the full 500-epoch run).

Per-epoch wall time over the full 500-epoch runs (3x A100, same node type). The fresh-stage-1 cost is largest right after the PB loss switches on and decays toward ~130 s as the model converges; isolated spikes are filesystem/solver-iteration jitter present in both modes.

The fresh solve is most expensive right after the PB loss switches on and relaxes as the model converges — the better the predicted density, the better the prior-only initial guess and the fewer solver iterations.

Accuracy does not pay for this: the PB entry is visibly smoother without the stale-cache cold start (epochs 30–33 average, validation: Fermi 0.130 vs 0.209 eV, Φ1D 0.140 vs 0.164 eV), and the 500-epoch endpoint comes out slightly ahead (potential 0.0665 vs 0.0713 eV, Fermi 0.0359 vs 0.0369 eV, Φ1D 0.0360 vs 0.0374 eV, validation). The lagged mechanism analysed above is therefore no longer used in production: every encounter is a "first encounter" by construction, and at the converged model the fresh solve reproduces the cached-field answers.
