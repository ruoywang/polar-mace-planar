Residual-3D Solvent Charge: Mechanism and Validation

On top of the 1-D Poisson–Boltzmann solvation model, a residual head completes the solvent bound and ionic charge on the full 3-D grid. This page answers four questions — how the prediction works, what it consumes, what it outputs, and how a grid value is assembled — followed by the design and results of three validation rounds.

4Supervision: labels and the point pack

5Probe I: can this representation hold the answer? (no training)

Principle: a trained model supplies baseline and envelopes; a per-frame least-squares fit against the DFT labels then gives the accuracy ceiling of a perfectly trained head. Nine frames (charged/neutral, held-out/train) agree.

6Probe II: can a network learn it? (frozen backbone)

Principle: freeze the backbone, train only a small head, and score it on 59 never-seen validation frames — the cross-structure learnability question. Three head architectures separate “architecture” from “feature information content”.

7The joint-training gate: 34 epochs, measured

A strict single-variable pair: w=1 vs w=0 (same code, seed and data; the configs differ by one weight line). The metric is validation sampled-point rms, normalized here by each channel’s warmup baseline.
