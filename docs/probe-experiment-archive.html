2 Compute cost: what point-wise evaluation really costs

The PB closure for charged frames needs the density on the full PB grid (100×100×300 = 3M points, ≈69M atom–point edges) before the Poisson solve. Per-frame inference cost on one A100:

The structural difference: DeepH-R's training loss is defined directly on sampled points (200 per structure per epoch) — the full grid is paid once, at inference. Our PB solve sits inside the training loop (potential and Fermi are per-step losses), so charged frames pay the full grid every step. That is the source of the 10× below — an architectural difference, not an implementation gap.

Quantitative closure for r1: residual dipole −0.025 eÅ ÷ area 172 Å² ≈ 26 meV potential offset, matching the observed 25.7 meV potential degradation. The 16×16 quadrature reproduces the r3 residual dipole to 0.05% against a 48×48×256 dense reference — quadrature accuracy is not a factor.

4.3 Debugging chain (chronological)

  1. r1 behind everywhere, including on the training set (rules out overfitting). A strictly larger hypothesis space should not do worse — treated as a defect and investigated.
  2. Post-training diagnosis: residual amplitude near zero; the entire density regression lives in the shared core. Three losses found not to include the residual's contribution: the scalar potential's dipole, the dipole correction inside the 1D-profile loss, and the 1D net-charge profile loss (effective weight 3.0, the heaviest density term). Fixed @abe48a8, @e870f49.
  3. Per-term gradient measurement (r2 mid-run checkpoint): the density losses deliver 4+ orders of magnitude less gradient to the probe head than to the GTO coefficients; the potential loss delivers normal magnitudes — the head is trained by the potential channel only.
  4. Root cause one: density_3d was the only density loss without signal-variance normalization (raw (e/Å³)² units, ≈4e-3 of the total loss). Root cause two: Adam's per-parameter step is dominated by the large signal; the density signal contributes <1e-6 of the update direction. Measured additionally: the output MLP's second hidden layer contracts to 1e-3–1e-5, further reducing density-gradient sensitivity. Normalization fix @3a7bb46.
  5. r3 (all fixes in): the residual still gains no 3D amplitude; its dipole instead grows to −0.55 eÅ. Structural conclusion: the dipole's leverage on the potential (× z × volume, ≈175× sensitivity) gives the potential losses far more control over the residual than the density loss has. A free-form residual in joint training is either recruited as a dipole corrector (potential sees it) or suppressed for carrying dipole (potential blind to it) — under neither visibility does 3D shaping happen.

5 Conclusions and options

Established: (1) the readout form's expressiveness advantage is real (v0: error ÷2.7 under identical supervision) but is realized only in the frozen-backbone post-processing setting; (2) in joint training a free-form residual cannot be shaped by the density loss because of the dipole leverage — three A/B rounds negative with the mechanism fully identified; (3) the charged-frame full-grid cost (10× per epoch) is independent of the above; production would additionally need the sized-up levers (half the projection width −30%, cutoff 5 Å −40%, coarse grid ÷8).
